Religious & Spiritual Emojis: Sacred Symbols & Cultural Context

By EmojiGuide Editorial Team • Published 2025-02-10 • 7 min read

Unicode's Commitment to Global Cultural Representation

The Unicode Consortium was established to ensure that all global writing systems and cultural symbols can be encoded cleanly across computing hardware. In recent years, proposals from international cultural organizations have expanded representations of South Asian, Middle Eastern, and indigenous spiritual customs.

The Sacred Diya Lamp 🪔 and Diwali

The Diya Lamp (🪔) was approved in Unicode 12.0 in 2019 following petitions to honor Diwali, the festival of lights celebrated by Hindus, Jains, and Sikhs. Lit with mustard oil or ghee and a cotton wick, the lamp signifies the triumph of spiritual wisdom over ignorance and hope over despair.

Om 🕉️ and Vedic Traditions

The Om symbol (🕉️) is the most revered sacred sound and cosmic icon in Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism. In digital communication, it is shared with deep reverence during auspicious ceremonies, prayer mornings, and reflective mindfulness practices.

Folded Hands 🙏: Prayer, Reverence and Cultural Etiquette

While occasionally used informally in western chats as a high five, the Folded Hands (🙏) glyph has its foundational roots in the Indian gesture of 'Namaste' ('I bow to the divine within you') and Japanese bowing etiquette ('itadakimasu' and 'arigatou'). Approaching these symbols with cultural understanding fosters global digital empathy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why did it take until 2019 for the Diya Lamp 🪔 to be added? ▾

New emojis require formal proposals submitted to the Unicode Emoji Subcommittee demonstrating widespread global demand, distinct visual iconography, and cultural permanence.

Can religious emojis be searched by their Unicode codepoints? ▾

Yes. On EmojiGuide, you can find any spiritual symbol using its code point (such as U+1F549 for Om or U+1FA94 for Diya Lamp).
